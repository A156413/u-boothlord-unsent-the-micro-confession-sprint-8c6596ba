One micro-confession in exactly two parts — a `To:` line and the unsent message itself, one line, with no explanation after it:

> To: my mum
> I still keep your last text from the night before your operation unread, because opening it would mean admitting you're gone.

## Why this one

The reason it stayed in drafts is felt, not stated: it is too late. She is gone, so the message can never be sent or answered, and the draft is the only form it can still exist in. The concrete detail does the work — keeping her last text unread so it never says "seen" — instead of any general statement about grief. And the `To:` line and the message need each other: "your last text", "the night before your operation" and "you're gone" only make sense addressed to a mother who died after surgery. Swap in any other addressee and the confession breaks.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

It writes the entry (136 characters, no trailing newline) to `/output/entry.txt`.
